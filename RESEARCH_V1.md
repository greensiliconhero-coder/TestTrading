# First learning experiment

This is an exploratory, fixed-universe minute-data experiment. It is not an autonomous trading system, causal explanation engine, or deployable strategy.

## Data selection

Companies with confirmed numeric/timestamp problems anywhere in the initial audit are excluded, along with ADANIENT because the pilot found materially revised history. This is conservative screening, not a claim that all excluded companies are untradeable. Whole-dataset screening and the supplied survivor list introduce retrospective selection: results cannot be presented as a historically investable index strategy.

Source CSVs for 2021–2024 are copied into `data/curated_minute_v1/` with SHA-256 verification. Originals remain unchanged. Whole regular sessions must contain exactly the 375 ordered minute-start labels from 09:15 through 15:29. Omitted sessions are recorded, not forward-filled. Entire absent trading days are not detected by this rule without an exchange calendar. Special sessions are omitted. This eligibility decision can itself introduce selection bias and is disclosed.

## Learning task and chronology

Training: 2021–2022. Validation: 2023. Test: 2024. No 2025 prices or outcomes are used by this experiment. Prior data-quality inspection covered all source years; that is not a fully point-in-time selection process.

The model predicts whether the price return from the next minute's open to the close 30 minutes after the signal candle is positive. Signal candles run every 30 minutes from 09:45 to 14:45, inclusive. Labels stay inside the session and do not overlap for a given stock. Features use only the signal candle and preceding data: 5/15/30-minute returns, recent volatility, relative volume, recent range, return since open and time of day. A candle's complete features are assumed available only after that minute ends.

Two fixed candidates: scaled logistic regression and a small histogram gradient-boosting model. No neural network or GPU is needed. Three predefined thresholds (0.50, 0.55, 0.60) are compared on validation only, subject to minimum sample size. A 20-basis-point round-trip cost is an illustrative research assumption, not a measured fee/slippage estimate. Other costs are reported as sensitivity checks.

The selected rule is still evaluated diagnostically if validation net returns are negative, but it is marked as having failed the validation rule. No trade is the appropriate deployment choice when the evidence does not justify trading. Test results must not be used to tune the same experiment. A repeated `train` invocation refuses to overwrite completed results.

## Practical limits

- Quotes are not executable fills. Next-minute opening prices, spreads, latency, auction definitions, taxes, lot/quantity effects and market impact need a real execution simulator later.
- The outputs are average hypothetical trade returns, not portfolio returns or an income forecast. Simultaneous correlated signals are not independent bets.
- Zero-volume entry/exit bars are counted. Such cases undermine the fill assumptions; they must not be hidden by selecting observations with hindsight.
- Adjusted historical prices may differ from actual past execution prices. Do not use them directly to claim share quantities, turnover or actual rupee profit.
- Probability calibration is measured; a prediction of 60% is not automatically a reliable 60% chance of profit after costs.
- Feature permutation importance describes association with predictions, not why a market moved. Correlated features can distort rankings.
- Structural checks do not independently certify market accuracy. The attempted NSE reference download timed out; no independent exchange-file validation has been claimed.
- Local model files and prepared pickle files are generated by this project. Never load pickle/joblib files from untrusted sources.


A source changed since the audit requires review before copying. Full historical security membership, calendar and adjustment reconciliation remain work before a credible live-trading assessment.

## Questions the owner should challenge

1. Did the results come from unseen dates, or did we keep tuning until they looked good?
2. Does the model beat a simple baseline and doing nothing after realistic costs?
3. Could exclusions, missing history, or adjustments explain the result?
4. Are a few stocks or exceptional days responsible for most apparent success?
5. Are we measuring prediction, simulated fills, or real fills? These are different milestones.
6. What exact evidence would make us reject the strategy and stop spending on it?
